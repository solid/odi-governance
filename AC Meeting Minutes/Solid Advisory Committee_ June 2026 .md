**Solid Advisory Committee: June 2026 — Meeting Summary**

***Individual contributions are not attributed.***

**Theory of Change**

The digital Theory of Change pyramid was presented as a central asset for defining priorities for Solid, with the aim of finalising and publishing it on the website so that project direction is easier to communicate to the community.

**Vision and mission alignment**

The group discussed whether the vision and mission statements need revisiting to ensure they remain current. A general consensus was reached on these statements previously, but the documentation needs updating to reflect the latest outcomes of the Theory of Change discussions.

**Engagement and roadmap**

It was emphasised that the Theory of Change represents the whole of Solid, not only the parts funded by the ODI, and that it needs broad contribution to be effective. The group agreed it is not yet ready for publication.

**Feedback mechanism and sessions**

On how to gather Advisory Committee input, participants expressed a preference for dedicated discussion sessions rather than individual comments on complex documents. Drop-in sessions across different time zones were proposed to make this easier. It was also suggested that participants could contribute bullet points on milestones and work items asynchronously ahead of those sessions.

**Level of ambition**

The group discussed whether goals should be realistic or highly ambitious. The consensus was to keep high ambition for the long-term vision while grounding the 12-month plan in operational realities.

**Community relational chart**

A draft chart mapping organisational relationships within the Solid community was presented, with the intention of publishing it on the Solid website. The chart aims to clarify the roles of the various groups, including the Advisory Committee, and the group was invited to comment on where different entities sit.

**Linked Web Storage placement**

A substantial part of the discussion concerned whether Linked Web Storage should sit inside or outside the Solid ecosystem boundary on the chart. Some participants argued that LWS is functionally part of the Solid progression; others noted that its status as a W3C standard makes the relationship distinct and more complex.

**Broadening inclusivity**

It was suggested that the chart should be welcoming to other groups working on distributed data problems, even where they fall outside the current Solid definition, and that it should show how diverse projects can work alongside core Solid efforts.

**Clarifying governance roles**

The group discussed the need to distinguish clearly between the advisory role of the Advisory Committee and the operational support provided by the ODI Solid team. There was agreement that the chart should show explicitly how different organisations, including future partners, can contribute to the community structure.

**Decision on chart strategy**

The chart will be iterated further, with areas still needing consensus highlighted, and shared for consultation before publication.

**Use cases framework**

A framework for selecting project use cases was introduced. Use cases must demonstrate measurable social impact and cover sectors such as health, volunteering and public services. Direct financial grants are not part of the offer; support will take the form of strategic input and in-kind resources.

**Selection process**

An open call for use cases is planned to launch in late August or early September and will remain open for four weeks. A scoring matrix will be used to assess applicants against criteria including social impact, technical sustainability and compatibility, with a judging panel providing oversight.

**Support offered to selected projects**

Planned support includes architecture workshops, implementation roadmaps, technical troubleshooting and mentoring, with resources allocated fairly across three selected projects over the course of the programme.

**Success metrics and risk tolerance**

The group discussed how to define success, noting that some high-value, socially beneficial projects may carry higher risk or need longer timelines than the programme allows. There was agreement that the selection criteria should balance these risks, and that the development of open-source tooling should itself count as a form of success, whether or not a given project reaches full deployment within the programme period.

**Hosting and custom domain names**

A question was raised about whether projects hosted on an ODI server instance could use their own domain names rather than an ODI domain. This is not currently supported, and building it as custom functionality is not planned. Hosting a separate server was raised as an alternative, with the caveat that it is hard to maintain without support. It was noted that a “bring your own domain” capability is in development for one of the other Solid server implementations. The hosting question will be followed up outside the meeting**.**
